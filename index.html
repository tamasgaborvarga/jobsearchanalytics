Methodology & framing

—

I treat my own job search the way I'd treat any business problem — collecting the data, measuring what matters and visualizing it, so decisions come from evidence rather than gut feel. I also do it to read the current job market and understand it better. Every number is computed live from my application log; company names are removed, only city and role title are shown.

How the metrics are calculated

  • Interview conversion = applications that reached an interview ÷ outbound applications sent. All interviews came from applications I initiated.
  • Response rate = applications that got any reply (interview invite or rejection) ÷ outbound applications — a yes/no measure.
  • Avg days to rejection uses only rejections where I have both the application date and the rejection-email date. Median sits well below the mean — most rejections are fast, a few drag the average up.
  • Status reflects the final state — an application that reached an interview but was later rejected counts as rejected, while still appearing in the interview funnel.
  • Ghosted = an outbound application still with no reply 30+ days after I applied. Since the median rejection lands in 10.5 days, a month of silence almost always means a silent no. Ghosted applications are split out of "awaiting", so the active pipeline isn't inflated by dead leads.

What I deliberately left out — no fake precision

Avg days to interview invite: I never logged interview-invite timestamps, so there is no honest number to show — I'm not estimating it.

  • The response rate can't separate a personal email from an automated rejection — I have no data flagging that, so I don't claim it.
  • The 30-day "ghosted" cutoff is my assumption, not a signal from the company — a few may still reply late. That's why I flag them as ghosted, never as a formal rejection.
  • No "fit score" or subjective quality rating, and no company names — only city + role title.
